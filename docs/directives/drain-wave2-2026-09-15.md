### The settled design — decided at this desk, do not redesign

lc-47 asked a two-way question (LIFT the closure lines into the
ledger line, or EXEMPT bodies carrying them). **Both were measured
and both were rejected.** Over the real done home: 47 of 49 DONE
bodies carry closure lines and 18 DROPPED carry none, so EXEMPT
spares every body holding the mass and leaves compaction reaching
only the short dropped ones; and LIFT merely relocates ~60k
characters (median `closed-reason` 1067, max 3722) out of the
carrier and into a ledger read chronologically.

**The answer is neither.** Compaction collapses a done body to a
ledger line carrying the item id, the `closed-ref`, AND **the blob
sha of the done carrier at closure time**, so the full text stays
recoverable by `git cat-file -p <blob>` while the carrier actually
shrinks. One home, moving from carrier to git-at-a-pin in one step.

**THE PIN IS THE VINTAGE BLOB, NEVER HEAD.** The sibling dotfiles
repo runs 412 line-anchored citations through this same pattern in
production, and it recorded the hazard: pinning HEAD landed 313 of
318 pointers on the wrong entry and NOTHING FAILED, because a line
number always resolves. A compaction pinning HEAD is worse than one
pinning nothing.

**The verb REFUSES to compact a body it would silently strip.** That
refusal is a registered row in `refusals.py` with its firing input
and a fire/control differing in ONE dimension (law 2, law 24 — which
is why `refusals.py` is in your write set at all; the desk had to
amend this item to add it).
