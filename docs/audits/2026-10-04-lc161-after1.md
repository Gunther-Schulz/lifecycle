# lc-161 AFTER-1: operator interventions after the 2026-09-24 ship set

**2026-10-04, desk lifecycle-4f.** The after arm for R1 to R3 of the first
refocus round (lc-286, lc-287, lc-288), run before anything from round 2
ships, as the judgment desk ordered (LEDGER, round 2 D6). Aggregates only;
raw operator text stays under
`$XDG_STATE_HOME/lifecycle/baselines/lc161-2026-10-04-after1-*`.

**Verdict: no drop. The gate question is discharged and nothing is
released.** The rate of operator messages roughly doubled. That rise is not
attributable to the ship set: five sessions carry 311 of the 514 messages,
four of them in one repo.

**Stated before the run** (round 2 D6): "no detectable change". That was
wrong. There is a detectable change and it points up.

## 1. The instrument

`tools/operator-interventions.py`, unchanged since the baseline, window
`--since 2026-09-25 --until 2026-10-04T17:00:00Z`.

Consistency control, run today: the same tool over the baseline window
(2026-09-10 to 2026-09-24T17:00Z) returns 394 sessions, 28,754 turns, 1,652
operator messages, 5.75 per 100 turns. Those are the baseline's own
figures, to the digit. So the two arms were read by the same instrument
over the same records.

Two things this table says:
- **The repo mix moved.** CachyOS-Setup carries 59% of the after window's

- **Five sessions carry 311 of the 514 messages**: four CachyOS-Setup

  CachyOS-Setup ones are the freeze arc already studied in

## 3. The classes, and why they are not comparable with the baseline

All 514 messages were classified, not a sample: three sonnet lanes, 172,
171 and 171 rows, interleaved in timestamp order so each lane saw the same
repo and time mix. Rubric quoted verbatim from the baseline's lane brief.
Coverage checked at the files: 514 of 514 rows, every index once, eleven
labels only.

| class | kind | n | share | baseline share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INFO | the operator's | 140 | 28.9% | 26.2% |
| STEER | target | 133 | 27.4% | 18.2% |
| CORRECTION | target | 57 | 11.8% | 19.6% |
| DECISION | the operator's | 47 | 9.7% | 12.9% |
| NUDGE | target | 43 | 8.9% | 7.0% |
| IGNITION | the operator's | 30 | 6.2% | 0.9% |
| RATIFICATION | target | 18 | 3.7% | 11.0% |
| RELAY | target | 8 | 1.6% | 1.2% |
| ALREADY-IN-RECORD | target | 5 | 1.0% | 1.6% |
| TASTE | the operator's | 4 | 0.8% | 1.4% |

n = 485 classified; 29 COULD-NOT-CLASSIFY. Targets: 264 of 485, 54.4%
(baseline 58.6%), which is 5.98 per 100 turns against 3.37.

**Read the shares as COULD NOT VERIFY at class grain.** Three measured
reasons:
- The after lanes marked 306 of 514 rows ambiguous (60%). The baseline's
  one lane marked 25 of 500 (5%). Same rubric, visibly different raters.
- The three lanes, given statistically equivalent thirds, disagree: INFO
  37, 61 and 42; COULD-NOT-CLASSIFY 8, 1 and 20. One lane put operator
  clarification questions in COULD-NOT-CLASSIFY, which the rubric reserves
  for harness text.
- The lanes say why, in their closing reports: the rubric has no label
  for a bare question from the operator ("what does this mean?", "do I
  need to restart?"), and each lane made its own policy. One put them in
  STEER under the "what is the goal?" clause and opening ones in IGNITION;
  one split them between STEER and NUDGE; one put them in
  COULD-NOT-CLASSIFY (17 of its 20 by its first report, 19 by its second). So part of STEER's rise and all of IGNITION's is a
  rubric gap being filled three ways, and how the baseline's single lane
  filled it is not on record.
- The target share is the stable part: 57%, 51% and 55% across the lanes.

So:
- **R1** (graded on ALREADY-IN-RECORD): 5 rows. COULD NOT VERIFY, as the
  baseline itself predicted for a class this small.
- **R3** (graded on STEER): the share rose from 18.2% to 27.4% and the rate
  from 1.05 to 3.01 per 100 turns. That is the opposite of the direction
  R3 hoped for. It is not evidence against R3 either: the goal print fired
  18 times in two sessions in this window (`tools/fire-window-tally.py`),
  and neither is among the five sessions that carry the rise.
- **The refocus as a whole** (targets per 100 turns): 3.37 to 5.98. No
  drop.

## 4. What this does and does not license

- It does not release the freeze. The release question asked for a drop;
  there is none.
- It does not show the ship set made things worse. R1 removed a notice
  nobody acted on and R3 printed a goal line 18 times. Neither plausibly
  doubles operator messages, and the rise is concentrated in sessions
  where R3 did not fire.
- It does show the measure is dominated by what kind of work is running.
  One hard debugging arc moved the machine-wide figure more than any
  mechanism in this repo is likely to. A before/after over all sessions
  cannot grade a mechanism of this size. Any later arm needs either a
  per-arc comparison or a much longer window.
- It is the clean BEFORE for whatever ships next, with that caveat
  attached.

## 5. Not verified

- No row was read by this desk beyond the coverage check and eight
  COULD-NOT-CLASSIFY bases. The baseline desk hand-graded 20 rows; this
  arm has no such grading yet.
- The figures come from the lanes' data files, read directly. Their
  closing reports arrived afterwards and their counts match the files
  (172, 171 and 171 rows; 113, 96 and 97 marked ambiguous). The lanes
  report no harness-generated row among the 514.
- Whether the three unnamed heavy CachyOS-Setup sessions are the freeze
  arc is inferred from the repo and dates, not opened.
