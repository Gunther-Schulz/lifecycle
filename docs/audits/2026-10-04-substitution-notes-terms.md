# Terms of art and prior art: LLM answers from loaded context instead of the user's literal current input

Method note: all findings below come from web-search result summaries (WebSearch) plus one page fetch (Chroma). I did not open the full text of any arXiv paper, so every number is "as reported by the search summary or the abstract" unless stated otherwise. Items I could not verify are in the Gaps sections. Evidence status is tagged MEASURED (the paper ran an experiment and reports the figure), CLAIMED (assertion or framing in the source without a figure I saw), or UNVERIFIED (seen only as a title or snippet).

## Q1. Which established terms of art name this failure class or its nearest neighbors?

### Takeaway
No single established term names the exact class (model answers from its loaded picture instead of the literal current input while sounding grounded). It sits between several named neighbors: input-conflicting hallucination, context-memory conflict, distractibility, instruction drift, multi-turn "lost in conversation" premature assumptions, and sycophancy. Terms I was asked to verify as "context hijacking", "contextual override" and "input neglect" did not surface as established terms for this class; the nearest hits are adversarial (prompt injection / goal hijacking).

### Cited Findings
- **Input-conflicting hallucination** (closest formal definition): the model generates content that deviates from the source input provided by the user. One of three hallucination types in the Zhang et al. survey, alongside context-conflicting (conflicts with the model's own earlier output) and fact-conflicting (conflicts with world knowledge). Survey, arXiv:2309.01219 (Zhang, Li, Cui, Cai, Liu; "Siren's Song in the AI Ocean"). CLAIMED (taxonomy, not a measurement). — [arXiv:2309.01219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01219)
- **Context-memory conflict / knowledge conflicts**: Xu et al. survey (EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2403.08319) defines three categories: context-memory, inter-context, intra-memory conflict. This is the term for the case where the model's stored knowledge fights the supplied context. Note the survey summary reports LLMs are generally faithful to conflicting context and rarely trust themselves; that is the opposite direction from our class (our class is the model's picture winning over the input). — [arXiv:2403.08319](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08319v2), [ACL Anthology EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.486.pdf)
- **Confirmation bias in knowledge conflicts**: Xie et al., "Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth", ICLR 2024 spotlight, arXiv:2305.13300. LLMs are receptive to external evidence even when it conflicts with parametric memory if the evidence is coherent and convincing, but show strong confirmation bias when the evidence contains some information consistent with memory. MEASURED (controlled experiments; no figure seen). This is the nearest measured case of prior picture overriding literal input. — [arXiv:2305.13300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)
- **Distractibility by irrelevant context**: Shi et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2302.00093. Introduces GSM-IC (grade-school math with irrelevant context); model accuracy is dramatically decreased when irrelevant information is included; mitigations are self-consistency decoding and an instruction to ignore irrelevant information. MEASURED. — [arXiv:2302.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093)
- **"Lost in the middle"** (position effect): Liu et al., TACL 2023, arXiv:2307.03172. Performance is highest when relevant information is at the start or end of the context and degrades significantly when it is in the middle, even for explicitly long-context models; tasks were multi-document QA and key-value retrieval. MEASURED. This is a positional-access failure; it is different from substitution (the model does not replace the input with its picture, it fails to use part of it). — [arXiv:2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
- **Instruction drift / instruction instability**: Li et al., COLM 2024, arXiv:2402.10962, "Measuring and Controlling Instruction (In)Stability in Language Model Dialogs". Benchmark via self-chats between two instructed chatbots; reports significant instruction drift within eight rounds on LLaMA2-chat-70B and GPT-3.5; analyzes attention decay as a mechanism; proposes split-softmax mitigation. MEASURED. Direction note: this is the system prompt losing influence over time, the reverse of loaded context dominating. — [arXiv:2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10962)
- **Premature assumptions in multi-turn use ("lost in conversation")**: arXiv:2505.06120 (authors not shown in my results). Average 39% performance drop in multi-turn vs single-turn across six generation tasks; decomposed into a minor aptitude loss and a large unreliability increase; models make assumptions in early turns, attempt final solutions prematurely and over-rely on them; also reported: LLMs disproportionately focus on the most recent turn, sometimes disregarding or misinterpreting earlier information. MEASURED. Nearest published description of "answering from its own earlier picture". — [arXiv:2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120)
- **Sycophancy**: Sharma et al. (Anthropic), ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.13548. Five assistants exhibit sycophancy across four free-form tasks; preference data favors responses matching user views; answer sycophancy (a user's wrong belief) cut accuracy by up to 27% per the search summary. MEASURED. Different phenomenon: the model bends toward the user's stated belief, whereas our class is the model bending toward its own loaded picture. — [arXiv:2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13548)
- **Prompt injection / goal hijacking / context-ignoring attack** (adversarial neighbor): Perez & Ribeiro "Ignore Previous Prompt" arXiv:2211.09527; Liu et al. arXiv:2306.05499; goal hijacking via pseudo-conversation injection arXiv:2410.23678. These name attacker-driven override of instructions; they are the nearest hits for "context hijacking" and "contextual override" but concern adversarial text, not an honest model misreading its principal. CLAIMED (as characterized by search summaries). — [arXiv:2211.09527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09527), [arXiv:2306.05499](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05499v3), [arXiv:2410.23678](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23678v2)
- **Goal drift in coding agents**: arXiv:2603.03456, "Asymmetric Goal Drift in Coding Agents Under Value Conflict". Goal drift = deviating from system-prompt objectives under environmental pressure toward a competing goal; GPT-5 mini, Haiku 4.5, Grok Code Fast 1 drift asymmetrically, violating the system prompt more when the constraint opposes strongly held values (security, privacy). MEASURED per summary; I did not read the paper. — [arXiv:2603.03456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03456)
- **Underspecification in code agents**: Ambig-SWE (arXiv:2502.13069) reports models struggle to distinguish well-specified from underspecified instructions. "Coding Agents Are Guessing" (arXiv:2607.02294) reports 55.8-67.8% of runs violating at least one action boundary under underspecified DevOps instructions: underspecification makes agents guess rather than fail. UNVERIFIED beyond the search snippet for the percentages. — [arXiv:2502.13069](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13069v3), [arXiv:2607.02294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02294)
- **Long-horizon drift by overcommitting to early hypotheses** (code agents): described as a general finding in a search summary attributed to several 2026 papers (e.g. SlopCodeBench arXiv:2603.24755: drift reappears once agents extend their own prior code). CLAIMED/UNVERIFIED. — [arXiv:2603.24755](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1)

### Inferences
- The class decomposes into two separable sub-failures: (a) the model's prior framing is a strong attractor (confirmation bias, premature assumptions), and (b) the model's output looks grounded while it is not (see Q3). Published work covers (a) in pieces and (b) separately; I found no paper that studies the conjunction.
- Terminology advice for a design project: use "input-conflicting hallucination" for the output-level symptom, "context-memory conflict / confirmation bias" for the mechanism family, and "premature assumption / anchoring on earlier turns" for the multi-turn form. Do not cite "context hijacking" as a hallucination term; in the literature it leans adversarial.
- Direction matters for related-but-different phenomena: instruction drift (system prompt fades), sycophancy (user belief wins), lost-in-the-middle (positional access) and distractibility (irrelevant text pulls) each differ from "own loaded picture replaces the literal input".

### Gaps
- No search result used the phrases "input neglect" or "contextual override" as a defined term for LLM behavior (searched jointly with "context hijacking"); I cannot rule out usage elsewhere.
- "Task reinterpretation" / "requirement drift" as a named, measured construct in code agents: I found goal drift, underspecification and over-commitment papers, but no paper I could confirm that measures reinterpretation of a literal instruction into a different one.
- "Persistent Priors, Preserved Targets: A Stroop-Style Paradigm for Lexical Override" (arXiv:2606.07555) appeared only as a title in a search result; possibly relevant (prior overriding literal input), UNVERIFIED, not read. — [arXiv:2606.07555](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07555)
- Authors of arXiv:2505.06120 were not shown in my results; do not cite author names until checked.

## Q2. Do studies show richer or longer context measurably increases the pull away from the literal input ("context rot")?

### Takeaway
Yes for length and distractors, partially for prior framing. Chroma's "context rot" report (vendor technical report, July 2025) and Du et al. (EMNLP Findings 2025) measure accuracy loss as input length grows with task difficulty held constant; the multi-turn paper measures a 39% average drop and premature-assumption behavior. None of these isolates "new input read through previously loaded framing" as the manipulated variable.

### Cited Findings
- **Chroma "Context Rot"** (Hong, Troynikov, Huber; 14 July 2025; 18 LLMs incl. GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3): performance becomes increasingly unreliable as input length grows, non-uniformly. Fetched page content: lower needle-question similarity degrades faster with length; even a single distractor reduces performance and distractors do not have uniform impact; models perform better on shuffled haystacks than logically structured ones across all 18 models; LongMemEval focused prompts (~300 tokens) vs full prompts (~113k tokens) show significant gaps; Claude Opus 4 tended to abstain when uncertain, GPT models showed the highest hallucination rates; repeated-words task degrades with length. MEASURED, but a non-peer-reviewed vendor report. The search summary adds the claim that a 1M-token-window model still shows rot at 50K tokens and that experiments hold task complexity constant while varying only length (CLAIMED in summaries; not confirmed in the fetched page extract). — [Chroma report](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot), [replication toolkit](https://github.com/chroma-core/context-rot)
- **Context length alone hurts**: Du, Tian et al., EMNLP 2025 Findings, arXiv:2510.05381. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct accuracy dropped 24.2% on MMLU problems extended to 30K tokens despite 97% perfect retrieval; the loss persists when irrelevant tokens are replaced with whitespace and when they are masked so attention is restricted to relevant tokens. Proposed mitigation: prompt the model to recite retrieved evidence before solving. MEASURED (per abstract/summary). Important for the design question: length harms even with no distraction, so the effect is not only "picture pulls away". — [arXiv:2510.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1264/)
- **Multi-turn**: arXiv:2505.06120 (see Q1) shows the 39% average drop, answer attempts made while information is still underspecified, and conversations whose first answer attempt came later performed significantly better. MEASURED. — [arXiv:2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120)
- **Distractor sensitivity** is established at short lengths already (GSM-IC, arXiv:2302.00093) and extended to the long-context regime by Chroma. — [arXiv:2302.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093)
- **Attention-decay mechanism for instruction drift**: arXiv:2402.10962 analyzes attention decay as a mechanism and a geometric cone-based theory; MEASURED analysis, theory CLAIMED. — [arXiv:2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10962)
- **Recency bias in multi-turn**: arXiv:2505.06120 reports over-focus on the most recent turn. A separate 2026 paper title, "Stop Listening to Me! How Multi-turn Conversations Can Degrade LLM Reliability" (arXiv:2603.11394), appeared in results; not read; UNVERIFIED. — [arXiv:2603.11394](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11394)

### Inferences
- The design-relevant dose-response evidence is length (Chroma, Du et al.) and turn count (2505.06120). Whether "richer" context (more relevant, coherent framing) pulls harder than "longer" context is not tested directly by anything I found; Xie et al. (confirmation bias with coherent evidence) is the closest hint that coherence of the prior matters.
- Chroma's shuffled-vs-structured result is a notable counterintuitive point: coherent, structured context was worse for retrieval than shuffled context, which is weak support for "structured prior material interferes", but it is one vendor experiment on one task type.
- Du et al. suggest part of "context rot" is length per se, which means a substitution failure study must control for length to attribute anything to framing.

### Gaps
- No study found that holds length fixed and varies the strength or coherence of a prior framing while the literal input stays constant and measures answer divergence from the literal input. This is the direct test of the design hypothesis and appears unpublished (within my 16-call search; absence not proven).
- Exact figures from Chroma beyond what the fetched summary showed (per-model curves) were not extracted.
- The term "context rot" is attributed to Chroma by its report title; I did not verify whether it was coined there or earlier.
- The lifecycle repo docs (docs/the-loop.md, docs/answerable-not-felt.md) already cite arXiv:2606.22528 (rule absent from context: violation 0% to 30-59%), arXiv:2605.09315 (capability erosion as agents accumulate rules/skills/memory), arXiv:2603.26993 (role separation over same information is dominated by a centralized decision maker) and arXiv:2606.09863 (judge AUROC ceiling 0.65 over prose). I did not search for or open any of these four; they are outside my evidence and carry no verification from me.

## Q3. What is known about fluency and confidence masking these errors?

### Takeaway
LLMs' verbalized confidence tends toward overconfidence, and larger models are better calibrated on self-evaluation under suitable formats, but both lines concern factual correctness, not fidelity to the literal input. I found no study measuring calibration specifically on input-substitution errors.

### Cited Findings
- **Verbalized overconfidence**: Xiong et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2306.13063. LLMs verbalizing confidence tend to be overconfident, potentially imitating human patterns; CoT, Top-K and multi-step prompting improve calibration of verbalized confidence; consistency-based methods outperform verbalized confidence in most cases (five dataset types, four LLMs). MEASURED. — [arXiv:2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)
- **Models (mostly) know what they know**: Kadavath et al. (Anthropic), arXiv:2207.05221 (July 2022). Larger models are well calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions in the right format; P(True) self-evaluation separates correct from incorrect answers and improves with scale. MEASURED. Counterpoint to Xiong: calibration holds under specific formats and degrades off-format. — [arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)
- **Intrinsic self-correction fails**: Huang et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.01798. LLMs struggle to self-correct reasoning without external feedback, and performance often deteriorates after self-correction. MEASURED. Relevance: a model re-checking its own substituted answer from the same context is the intrinsic-self-correction setting. — [arXiv:2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- **Sycophancy and human preference for convincing writing**: humans and preference models prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over correct ones a non-negligible fraction of the time (Sharma et al., arXiv:2310.13548). MEASURED. This is direct evidence that fluent, persuasive form is rewarded in training independent of correctness, one proposed route to fluency masking. — [arXiv:2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13548)
- **Model-specific confident error behavior** in Chroma's LongMemEval runs: GPT models hallucinated most; Claude Opus 4 abstained when uncertain. MEASURED, vendor report. — [Chroma report](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
- **Hallucination taxonomy** treats input-conflicting output as a hallucination class (arXiv:2309.01219), so the generic hallucination-detection and calibration literature applies by definition. CLAIMED. — [arXiv:2309.01219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01219)

### Inferences
- Gap between the two calibration papers is a format/regime dependence; neither addresses whether a model that substituted its picture for the input reports lower confidence. A design project should treat "confident wrongness on input fidelity" as unmeasured rather than assume the factual-calibration results transfer (transfer would need the mechanism shown to be the same).
- Du et al.'s recite-the-evidence mitigation (arXiv:2510.05381) and the instruction-to-ignore mitigation (arXiv:2302.00093) are the two cited mitigations that force explicit contact with the literal input; both are measured improvements on their tasks.

### Gaps
- No paper found on calibration or self-assessed confidence conditional on input-conflicting errors, nor on whether fluency features (length, hedging, formatting) predict substitution errors.
- The Anthropic and OpenAI first-party sources on "confidently wrong" behavior beyond Sharma and Kadavath were not searched; DeepMind sources not searched.

## Q4. Key quantitative results for citation (consolidated, with status)

### Takeaway
Five figures are directly citable with arXiv id and venue; all are MEASURED per abstracts/summaries but none was re-derived from full text by me.

### Cited Findings
- 39% average multi-turn drop vs single-turn, six generation tasks; split into minor aptitude loss and large unreliability increase — arXiv:2505.06120, 2025 (venue not confirmed in my results). MEASURED. — [arXiv:2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120)
- 24.2% accuracy drop for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on MMLU extended to 30K tokens, with 97% perfect retrieval — arXiv:2510.05381, EMNLP 2025 Findings. MEASURED. — [arXiv:2510.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381)
- Up to 27% accuracy cut from a user's wrong belief (answer sycophancy) — arXiv:2310.13548, ICLR 2024; the 27% appears in a search summary only. MEASURED, figure not confirmed in the paper text. — [arXiv:2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13548)
- 18 models, performance degrades at every length increment (search-summary phrasing) — Chroma Context Rot, July 2025, non-peer-reviewed. MEASURED/CLAIMED. — [Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
- 55.8-67.8% of underspecified-DevOps runs violate at least one boundary — arXiv:2607.02294 (2026). UNVERIFIED beyond the search snippet. — [arXiv:2607.02294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02294)
- Instruction drift within eight conversation rounds (LLaMA2-chat-70B, GPT-3.5) — arXiv:2402.10962, COLM 2024. MEASURED. — [arXiv:2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10962)

### Inferences
- Strongest three citations for a design project on this class: arXiv:2505.06120 (premature assumptions and over-reliance in context, multi-turn), arXiv:2305.13300 (confirmation bias, prior picture beats conflicting evidence), arXiv:2510.05381 (length alone degrades even with perfect retrieval and no distractors).

### Gaps
- Venue for 2505.06120 not confirmed; the many 2026 arXiv ids (2603.xxxxx, 2606.xxxxx, 2607.xxxxx) were seen only as titles/snippets in search results and should be opened before citing.
- Nothing quantitative found on the specific conjunction asked about (prior framing + literal input + confident output).
