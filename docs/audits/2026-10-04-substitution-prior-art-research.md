# Substitution has no name, but its parts do

The literature has no established term for "the model answers its loaded picture instead of the user's actual input", and no study isolates that conjunction. The failure decomposes into neighbors that are each measured: earlier context interferes with the newest input, a model's own early assumptions anchor later turns, self-review inherits the frame it should check, and fluent output evades human review. The closest published description is the multi-turn paper, which reports a **39% average performance drop** from single-turn to multi-turn, caused mostly by unreliability rather than lost aptitude, with models making early assumptions and not recovering from them ([Laban et al., arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)). Practitioners report the cost widely but name it differently ("ignores my CLAUDE.md", "almost right", "babysitting"), and only as anecdote. Mitigation evidence supports the lifecycle thesis in direction: checks against external state beat model self-review, and loaded rule files show no reliable gain. Two parts of the thesis are unmeasured in everything the notes found: injecting the record at a seam versus loading it at session start, and demanded read-backs. Lifecycle should therefore treat its mechanism as a design resting on adjacent evidence and measure it. Most figures below come from abstracts and search summaries, not full-text reads by the note authors, and each claim carries its grade.

## The nearest names describe one half each

No established term covers the class. The closest formal label is **input-conflicting hallucination**, output that deviates from the source the user supplied, one of three types in a hallucination survey ([Zhang et al., arXiv 2309.01219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01219)); that is a taxonomy, not a measurement. Terms such as "context hijacking", "contextual override" and "input neglect" did not surface as defined terms for this behavior; the hits that exist are adversarial, prompt injection and goal hijacking ([Perez and Ribeiro, arXiv 2211.09527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09527)). The practitioner notes found the same absence: no source described the behavior as paraphrase or substitution of the user's words, and "babysitting" mostly means watching permission prompts and long-running agents, so it is a poor search key for this problem.

Five measured neighbors each differ from substitution in direction. Knowledge-conflict work shows models are generally faithful to conflicting context, the opposite pull ([Xu et al., arXiv 2403.08319](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08319v2)), though they show **confirmation bias** when evidence partly agrees with memory, the nearest measured case of a prior picture beating literal input ([Xie et al., arXiv 2305.13300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)). Instruction drift is the system prompt fading within eight dialogue rounds, again the reverse direction ([Li et al., arXiv 2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10962)). Sycophancy is the model bending toward the user's stated belief, not toward its own picture ([Sharma et al., arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13548)). Lost-in-the-middle is a positional access failure rather than replacement ([Liu et al., arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)). Distractibility by irrelevant context is measured on math problems ([Shi et al., arXiv 2302.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093)). The terminology note's advice, an inference, is to use input-conflicting hallucination for the symptom, context-memory conflict and confirmation bias for the mechanism family, and premature assumption for the multi-turn form. The notes differ on the multi-turn paper's provenance: the terminology note could not show its authors or venue, the practitioner note names Laban, Hayashi, Zhou and Neville of Microsoft Research and Salesforce Research, and no note confirms the venue.

## Earlier context measurably competes with the newest input

The strongest mechanism evidence is proactive interference. When related key-value updates stream in and only final values are queried, accuracy falls log-linearly toward zero as prior updates accumulate; the errors are retrievals of overwritten values even though the final values sit right before the query, and prompt-engineering mitigations helped little ([Unable to Forget, arXiv 2506.08184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08184)). That is substitution in miniature, though the earlier material is stale data, not a framing. Length alone also hurts: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct lost **24.2% accuracy** on MMLU problems extended to 30K tokens despite 97% perfect retrieval, and the loss persisted when padding was whitespace or masked ([Du et al., arXiv 2510.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381), from abstract and summary). Any attribution to framing must therefore control for length. Chroma's report, vendor-authored and not peer reviewed, measured 18 models degrading as input grows, with distractors compounding the effect and logically structured haystacks performing worse than shuffled ones ([Chroma, Context Rot](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)); the structured-versus-shuffled result is one task type from one vendor, a weak hint that coherent prior material interferes.

The multi-turn paper adds the model's own earlier output as the competing material. It reports premature answer attempts built on early assumptions, over-reliance on earlier incorrect attempts, and over-weighting of first and last turns, with these behaviors cited from its sections via a summary rather than a full read ([arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06120)). Temperature zero did not fix it. The notes disagree on recap and snowball prompting: the mechanisms note reports modest gains that still trailed single-turn full-instruction performance, while the mitigations note could not see those tests, so treat that detail as single-source. The inference tying these results together, that richer context pulls harder because earlier material competes for a fixed attention budget and the model's own turns are conditioned on as ground truth, is a synthesis no paper states. No study holds length fixed and varies the strength or coherence of a prior framing while measuring divergence from the literal input; within the notes' search budgets that direct test appears unpublished, though absence is not proven. A Stroop-style paradigm for lexical override ([arXiv 2606.07555](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07555)) may bear on it but was seen only as a title and is unverified.

## Fluent output hides the failure from models and from people

Preference training gives partial support for why wrong output sounds grounded. Matching the user's views is among the most predictive features of human preference, and humans and preference models sometimes prefer convincing sycophantic answers over correct ones ([Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)); the "up to 27% accuracy cut" figure attached to this paper appeared only in a search summary and is unconfirmed. RLHF also made models better at convincing time-constrained evaluators without improving task performance, raising evaluator false-positive rates by 24.1% on question answering and 18.3% on coding ([Wen et al., arXiv 2409.12822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12822)); its PDF header says under review. Calibration evidence does not reach the case: verbalized confidence tends to be overconfident ([Xiong et al., arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)), while larger models are well calibrated in certain answer formats ([Kadavath et al., arXiv 2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)), and both concern factual correctness, not fidelity to the input. The inference that a substituted answer reads as correct to any reviewer who holds the same frame, because the frame supplies the high-probability text, has no direct citation.

The practitioner side corroborates a cost but cannot grade it. The Stack Overflow 2025 survey reports **66% of developers** call AI answers "almost right but not quite" and 45% lose significant time debugging AI code, read from secondary sources that agree with each other ([Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/developer-ai-trust-gap-adoption-vs-confidence)). The METR trial of 16 experienced developers found tasks took 19% longer with AI while participants believed they were about 20% faster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), search summary; METR has since labeled the result historical). That perception gap means self-reported cost or benefit, the babysitting complaint included, is testimony and not measurement. DORA 2025 found no measurable effect of AI on burnout, which conflicts at face value with the blogs reporting drain; the note author did not test why ([Faros summary](https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025), aggregator). The Berkeley Haas study of about 200 employees reports intensified work and fatigue, but at one company and outside a coding-agent setting ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it)).

The only first-hand reports of the specific mechanism are one GitHub issue (n=1) saying CLAUDE.md instructions are ignored after about ten turns ([anthropics/claude-code 98870](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/98870)), and an 80-comment Hacker News thread where users describe long files being ignored, adherence strongest at the start and end, and a user-built canary: a required marker at the start of every message, whose absence signals the rules have lapsed ([HN 46102048](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46102048)). The canary is the only practitioner pattern that detects the failure from outside the model's own judgment. Reddit, Cursor, Copilot and aider were not covered. The claim that this cost falls on ADHD or autistic users specifically found no support; the one fetched account describes imposed assumptions about how the user should work, not paraphrase of their words ([Furze](https://leonfurze.com/2026/02/22/lived-experience-using-ai-as-an-audhd-adult/)).

## Checks against state work; loaded rules and self-review do not

The most consistent pattern in the mitigation evidence is that interventions adding an independent signal beat same-context, same-frame checks. Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback does not reliably help and sometimes degrades reasoning ([Huang et al., arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)), and a critical survey finds reliable gains only where external feedback exists ([Kamoi et al., arXiv 2406.01297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01297)). Models also miss errors in their own output that they fix when attributed to the user, a **64.5% average blind-spot rate** across 14 non-reasoning models, which appending "Wait" cut by 89.3% ([Tsui, arXiv 2507.02778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02778)); that shows capability present but not elicited, not that per-turn exhortation works on frame substitution. An LLM judge reading prose reached at most 0.65 AUROC at detecting false success on one agent benchmark and 0.54 on another, while TF-IDF detectors over the same trajectories reached 0.83 and 0.95, because judges rely on confident closing language rather than verified state ([arXiv 2606.09863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863), fetched); the result covers false-success detection, not judging in general. Fresh-context review is the weakest adopted idea: one fetched study found a fresh-session reviewer scored F1 28.6% against 21.7% for repeated self-review, but not significantly better than single same-session review (27.1%, p=0.26) or subagent review, and the authors retracted an earlier claim that the ranking held in every run ([arXiv 2603.12123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12123)). The mechanisms note held only a snippet of this paper and described it as "proposed to improve"; the mitigations note's fetched reading governs.

On the loaded-rules leg, the one controlled study found repository context files such as AGENTS.md give **no reliable success gain** and raise inference cost over 20% on average; agents followed the instructions, but the extra exploration distracted them ([Gloaguen et al., arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988), fetched). Instruction density degrades following in a related test, where the best of 20 models reached 68% at 500 simultaneous instructions and omission was the dominant error ([IFScale, arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11538), snippet). On the persisted-record leg the evidence is vendor-sourced: a structured summary with fixed sections scored 3.70 of 5 against 3.44 and 3.35 for two built-in compressors, with every method weakest on artifact and file tracking at 2.19 to 2.45, as judged by an LLM ([Factory](https://factory.com/news/evaluating-compression), fetched, vendor). The cleanest evidence for a hard environment-side gate is modest: SWE-agent's edit command rejecting lint-failing edits recovered about 3 percentage points ([SWE-agent, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/5a7c947568c1b1328ccc5230172e1e7c-Paper-Conference.pdf), snippet). One caution applies to demand shape: constrained output formats hurt reasoning in one study, which argues for making a demand a separate write step rather than a schema imposed inside the reasoning ([Tam et al., arXiv 2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442), snippet).

## What the evidence grades for the lifecycle thesis

The thesis has four legs: loaded rules stay inert, self-correction degrades, the environment must demand the record at seams, and reset from carriers beats compaction. The first two have measured support; the last two rest on adjacent evidence. The table grades each learning with the notes' honesty labels. Three arXiv ids cited in the repo's own docs (2606.22528, 2605.09315, 2603.26993) were not opened by any note author and carry no verification here.

| Learning for lifecycle | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Do not rely on always-loaded rules for fidelity | Moderate, measured | AGENTS.md null result (fetched, [2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)); IFScale density and length degradation (snippet); the repo's cited jump in violations from 0% to 30-59% when a rule is absent from context ([2606.22528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528), repo-sourced, unverified here) |
| Self-review inherits the frame, so checks must come from outside it | Strong, measured | Huang, Kamoi, Tsui blind spot, false-success AUROC (fetched); none tests substitution specifically |
| Checks against state or tool output beat judges over prose | Strong, measured | Same studies plus SWE-agent lint (modest, one study) |
| Fixed named slots beat free-form regenerated summaries | Moderate-weak | Factory only: vendor, LLM-judged, compares summarizers, not record-keeping |
| Environment-side demands at the seam beat session-start loading | Unmeasured | Adjacent only: multi-time retrieval in RAG ([arXiv 2406.12534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12534), secondary snippet), position effects, IFScale; FLARE and Self-RAG trigger on model-side signals, with no counterpart to an environment-side trigger |
| Demanded read-back or verbatim quote, checked mechanically | Unmeasured | Re-reading and rephrasing help benchmark reasoning ([Re2](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.871/), snippet), a different task; Tsui's attribution effect is one paper on non-reasoning models |
| Restart from persisted carriers beats compaction | Unmeasured | No controlled reset-versus-compaction study; two unopened 2026 preprints, [2608.16370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16370) and [2608.13867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13867), may bear on it |
| Fresh-context review as a general fix | Unproven | Cross-context review: run-unstable, not significant against single self-review |

Three design cautions follow from the evidence rather than from the thesis. A seam that relies on the model noticing it needs the record inherits the fluency problem, because confident wrongness on input fidelity is unmeasured and felt insufficiency is exactly what fluency suppresses; the environment must fire the demand. Length harms even without distractors, so shrinking the always-loaded slice to the entries relevant at a decision is supported on length grounds whatever the framing mechanism turns out to be. And the demand should be a separate write step, because rigid formats inside reasoning can lower reasoning quality.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the problem more than it settles it. Substitution is not a rediscovered known failure but a composite whose pieces each have measurements pointing the same way, with no experiment crossing them. That cuts both ways for lifecycle: the thesis is consistent with every adjacent result, and nothing published tests it, so the project's own measurement is the missing evidence. The experiment the gaps imply holds length fixed, varies the coherence of a loaded framing, and compares seam-time injection of the record against session-start loading on divergence from the literal input or the record. Its outcome discriminates: parity would mean the seam adds nothing over presence, while a gap would settle the demand leg.

The practitioner record carries one further lesson. Self-reported cost and benefit are unreliable in both directions, as the METR perception gap shows, and the vendor-heavy sources have an incentive to name problems and sell fixes. The claim that babysitting is the per-message tax of substitution has no measured support in these notes; it rests on the operator's distilled observation and on anecdotes about ignored instruction files. A canary-style mechanical detector, which needs no one's testimony, is both the cheapest instrument and the best source of the missing measurement.
